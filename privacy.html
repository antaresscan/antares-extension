Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 16, 2026 · Effective from install

1. Overview

Antares (“the Extension”) is a browser extension that scans Solana token addresses on supported trading platforms and returns a risk assessment. This policy explains exactly what we process, which third parties are involved, where data goes, how long it is kept, and the rights you can exercise.

The short version: without an account, we process the Solana contract addresses you look at, the technical metadata any HTTPS service inherently sees (your IP, your browser user-agent), a random install identifier, and a history of the scans made with that identifier. If you create an account or buy a plan on antaresscan.com, we also process your e-mail address and the record of your purchase (sections 3.5 and 3.6). We do not collect your name, your wallet or private keys, or your browsing history beyond the single page where an address is detected.

2. Who we are

The Extension is operated by the COMEALAMAISONGROUPE project. Bug reports and feature requests go through antaresscan.com/support. For privacy questions, see the Contact section.

3. Data we process

3.1 Solana contract addresses

When the Extension detects a Solana contract address (base58, 32–44 characters) on a supported page, that address is sent to our API (antares-extension.vercel.app/api/scan) together with the request. The address is public blockchain data. Our legal basis for processing it is legitimate interest: it is the minimum data needed to deliver the risk assessment the user explicitly requested.

3.2 Technical request metadata

Any HTTPS request to our API is received via Vercel's edge network. Vercel sees and may log:

  • Your IP address (used transiently for rate limiting in Upstash Redis and for platform abuse protection).
  • A user-agent string (standard browser header).
  • Timestamp and URL of the request.

We do not use this metadata to identify you, for analytics, for profiling, or for advertising. In our own logs, an e-mail address is never written in clear: only a short one-way reference appears.

3.3 Install identifier

If the Extension sends an X-Antares-Install header (a random opaque identifier generated by your browser on first install), we use it together with your network address as a rate-limit key, so multiple users sharing a corporate NAT each get a fair quota. On its own the identifier says nothing about you. It is also the key under which we keep your scan history (3.4) and, if you have a plan, the plan attached to your install. If you sign in to an account or redeem a licence, the identifier is linked to your e-mail address. You can see it on the Extension's Options page, and you can start again with a new one by uninstalling and reinstalling the Extension.

3.4 Scan history

For each scan made with an install identifier we record the contract address, the token's symbol and name, the verdict and score, and the time of the scan. We keep about the 1,000 most recent scans per install. The history view only shows the last 30 days, but older entries are not erased at 30 days: they stay until newer scans push them out, or until the data is deleted (section 9).

3.5 Account (optional)

You only need an account to use a paid plan across your devices. If you create one on antaresscan.com we store: your e-mail address, your password as a salted one-way hash (we cannot read your password), the date of creation, a random account identifier, and a session counter. We use them to sign you in and to give you access to the licences bought with your e-mail. Legal basis: performance of the contract between you and us.

When you sign in, our API sets one cookie, antares_session (HttpOnly, valid 30 days), which holds a signed session token. The website also hands a copy of this token to the Extension, which keeps it in its own storage so the overlay can show your plan. Signing out ends the session in that browser; deleting your account ends every session at once (section 9.1).

To protect accounts against password guessing we keep, for 15 minutes, a counter of failed sign-ins under a one-way hash of the e-mail address. Legal basis: legitimate interest (security).

3.6 Purchases (optional)

Plans are paid in cryptocurrency through NOWPayments. When you start a checkout we create a checkout record: a reference, the plan, the price, the e-mail you gave, your install identifier, and the payment identifiers and transaction hash that NOWPayments reports. The checkout record is deleted automatically after 7 days. We send NOWPayments the price, our order reference and a description; we do not send your e-mail address. NOWPayments collects what it needs to process the payment, under its own policy.

When a payment is confirmed we create a licence record: the licence key, your e-mail address, the plan, the amount, the dates, the payment reference and, once you redeem the licence, the install identifier that redeemed it. This is the accounting record of the sale. We keep it for as long as the law requires us to keep accounting records, and it is not erased when you delete your account (section 9). Legal basis: performance of the contract and legal obligation.

3.7 What we do NOT collect

  • Your name, phone number or postal address.
  • Wallet addresses or private keys. The Extension never connects to your wallet.
  • Full browsing history — only pages on the supported platforms listed below.
  • Advertising or analytics trackers. The Extension itself sets no cookies; the only cookie is the sign-in cookie described in 3.5.

6. International transfers

Our infrastructure (Vercel, Upstash, Sentry) operates primarily from data centres in the United States. If you access the Extension from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, technical request metadata and, if you use an account, your account data are transferred outside your region. We rely on the processors' Standard Contractual Clauses where available. You can find each processor's transfer mechanism in their privacy policy linked above.

7. Data retention

  • Cache (Upstash Redis): adaptive TTL between 20 seconds (fast-moving new tokens) and 30 minutes (tokens already flagged RUG). Never indefinite. For up to 6 hours we also keep, per token, the last good answer of some of our data sources, and use it only when that source is down.
  • Rate-limit counters (Upstash Redis): sliding windows of 10 and 60 seconds. Counters expire automatically.
  • Rug database (Upstash Redis): contract addresses of tokens we have already flagged as RUG or DANGER, kept as a lookup index. No user data is stored in this dataset.
  • Scan history (Upstash Redis): about the 1,000 most recent scans per install identifier, until you delete your account or ask us to delete it (section 3.4).
  • Account (Upstash Redis): until you delete it (section 9). Sign-in sessions last 30 days at most; deleting the account ends all of them immediately.
  • Failed sign-in counter: 15 minutes.
  • Checkout records: 7 days.
  • Purchase records (licences): for as long as the law requires us to keep accounting records (section 3.6).
  • API access logs (Vercel): per Vercel's retention (typically hours to days).
  • Error reports (Sentry): per Sentry's default retention (90 days) unless the maintainer deletes them sooner.
  • On-device (Chrome storage): user preferences (theme, stealth toggle, recent scan list) and, if you signed in, the session token the website gave the Extension. They stay on your machine until you sign out, uninstall or clear extension data.

8. Chrome permissions

The Extension requests the following permissions, declared in package.json:

  • activeTab — run the content script on the current tab when a supported site is active.
  • storage — save your preferences and recent scan history locally.
  • notifications — optionally surface a DANGER verdict as a native OS notification.
  • alarms — schedule the service worker periodically so Manifest V3 does not tear it down mid-scan.
  • tabs — open the full-analysis page in a new tab when the user clicks through.
  • host_permissions — only the 8 trading platforms enumerated in section 4.

9. Your rights

Under GDPR (EEA / UK / Switzerland), CCPA (California), and similar laws, you have the right to:

  • Access — request a copy of any data tied to you.
  • Rectification — ask us to correct inaccurate data.
  • Erasure — ask us to delete your data.
  • Restriction — ask us to stop using your data while a dispute is resolved.
  • Portability — ask for your data in a common, machine-readable format.
  • Objection — object to processing based on legitimate interest.
  • Complaint — file a complaint with your local data-protection authority (e.g. the CNIL in France, the ICO in the UK).

9.1 Delete your account yourself

On your account page at antaresscan.com, choose Delete account and confirm with your password. This erases:

  • your account (e-mail address, password hash, session counter);
  • the link between your installs and your e-mail address;
  • the scan history and the plan stored on those installs.

Every session ends immediately, in every browser and in the Extension. What we keep is the purchase record (3.6): the licence key, your e-mail address, the plan, the amount and the dates, because the law requires us to keep accounting records. If you later create an account with the same e-mail address, your licences are available again.

9.2 Without an account

If you have no account, the data we hold about you is the scan history and the rate-limit counters tied to your install identifier. Copy the identifier from the Extension's Options page and email us (section 12): we delete the history held under it. Uninstalling the Extension and clearing its data removes what stays on your device.

9.3 Other requests

To access your data, correct it, receive it in a portable format, or exercise any other right, email the address in section 12 with a description of the right you are exercising. We will respond within 30 days.
